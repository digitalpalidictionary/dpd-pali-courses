# The Difference Between atthi and hoti

Both *atthi* (√as) and *hoti* (√hū) are translated as “is”. In many sentences one cannot simply replace the other. This page gives a working rule, examples from the suttas, and the limits of the rule.

## The Working Rule

| | atthi | hoti |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Basic sense | there is; exists; is present | is (something); becomes; happens |
| The question it answers | Is there X? | What is X like? What happens? |
| Usual place in the sentence | first | last |
| Negative | natthi | na hoti |

In short: *atthi* says **that** something is there. *hoti* says **what** something is, or what it comes to be.

## atthi: there is

### Existence

**atthi**, bhikkhave, ajātaṃ abhūtaṃ akataṃ asaṅkhataṃ ([UD73](https://s.dhamma.gift/kn/ud/index.html#ud8.3))

**There is**, monks, an unborn, unbecome, unmade, unconditioned.

**natthi** rāgasamo aggi ([DHP202](https://s.dhamma.gift/kn/dhp/index.html#dhp202))

**There is no** fire like lust.

### Presence here and now

‘**atthi** me ajjhattaṃ kāmacchando’ti pajānāti ([MN10](https://s.dhamma.gift/mn/mn10/index.html))

He knows: “**There is** sensual desire in me.”

‘**natthi** me ajjhattaṃ kāmacchando’ti pajānāti ([MN10](https://s.dhamma.gift/mn/mn10/index.html))

He knows: “**There is no** sensual desire in me.”

### Having

Pāḷi has no verb “to have”. The owner stands in the genitive or dative case, and *atthi* does the rest.

puttā **matthi** dhanaṃ **matthi** ([DHP62](https://s.dhamma.gift/kn/dhp/index.html#dhp62))

puttā + me + **atthi**, dhanaṃ + me + **atthi**

lit. sons of me there are, wealth of me there is

“**I have** sons, **I have** wealth”

## hoti: is, becomes, happens

### What someone or something is like

*hoti* is the usual verb in general descriptions, such as the lists of qualities of a monk or a disciple.

idha, mahānāma, ariyasāvako saddho **hoti** ([MN53](https://s.dhamma.gift/mn/mn53/index.html))

Here, Mahānāma, a noble disciple **is** faithful.

### Becoming

imasmiṃ sati idaṃ **hoti** ([SN12.21](https://s.dhamma.gift/sn/sn12/index.html#sn12.21))

When this is, that **comes to be**.

imasmiṃ asati idaṃ **na hoti** ([SN12.21](https://s.dhamma.gift/sn/sn12/index.html#sn12.21))

When this is not, that **does not come to be**.

Both verbs are in this sentence. The word *sati* here is not “mindfulness”. It is the locative of *santa*, the present participle of *atthi*: “when this is there”. The condition is simply present (√as), and the result comes to be (√hū).

### Happening

tassa mayhaṃ, bhikkhave, etad**ahosi** ([MN26](https://s.dhamma.gift/mn/mn26/index.html))

etaṃ + **ahosi**

lit. monks, to me there was this

“Monks, this thought **occurred** to me”

This is the common way to say that somebody had a thought.

### The background of a story

In stories *hoti* often stands with a past participle and describes the situation at that time. The verb is in the present tense, but the translation is in the past.

tena kho pana samayena poṭṭhapādo paribbājako mahatiyā paribbājakaparisāya saddhiṃ nisinno **hoti** ([DN9](https://s.dhamma.gift/dn/dn9/index.html))

At that time the wanderer Poṭṭhapāda **was** sitting together with a large assembly of wanderers.

## Both Verbs in One Sentence

‘**atthi** kāyo’ti vā panassa sati paccupaṭṭhitā **hoti** ([MN10](https://s.dhamma.gift/mn/mn10/index.html))

Or his mindfulness that “**there is** a body” **is** established.

*atthi* says that the body is there. *hoti* says in what state the mindfulness is.

## “Is” Without Any Verb

The most common way to say “X is Y” in Pāḷi uses no verb at all.

sabbe saṅkhārā aniccā ([DHP277](https://s.dhamma.gift/kn/dhp/index.html#dhp277))

All conditioned things **are** impermanent.

Do not expect *atthi* or *hoti* in every sentence that has “is” in the English translation.

## Two Roots, One Verb

The two roots also work together as one verb “to be”. √as has no future tense, so the future always comes from √bhū, the full form of √hū.

| Time | Verb | Root |
| --- | --- | --- |
| past | ahosi / āsi | √hū / √as |
| present | atthi / hoti | √as / √hū |
| future | bhavissati | √bhū |

One sutta names the three times with exactly these three words:

yaṃ, bhikkhave, rūpaṃ atītaṃ niruddhaṃ vipariṇataṃ ‘**ahosī**’ti tassa saṅkhā ([SN22.62](https://s.dhamma.gift/sn/sn22/index.html#sn22.62))

Monks, form that is past, ceased and changed is called “**it was**”.

yaṃ, bhikkhave, rūpaṃ jātaṃ pātubhūtaṃ, ‘**atthī**’ti tassa saṅkhā ([SN22.62](https://s.dhamma.gift/sn/sn22/index.html#sn22.62))

Monks, form that is born and has appeared is called “**it is**”.

yaṃ, bhikkhave, rūpaṃ ajātaṃ apātubhūtaṃ, ‘**bhavissatī**’ti tassa saṅkhā ([SN22.62](https://s.dhamma.gift/sn/sn22/index.html#sn22.62))

Monks, form that is not born and has not appeared is called “**it will be**”.

The sutta does not set *atthi* against *hoti*. It takes the past and the future from √hū / √bhū and the present from √as, as parts of one verb.

## The Limits of This Distinction

The working rule describes what is usual. It is not a law, and no teaching should be built on it.

**1. *atthi* is not only for what is seen directly.** It is also the verb for things one cannot see, and for views about existence.

**atthi** ayaṃ loko, **atthi** paro loko ([MN117](https://s.dhamma.gift/mn/mn117/index.html))

**There is** this world, **there is** another world.

‘sabbam**atthī**’ti kho, kaccāna, ayameko anto ([SN12.15](https://s.dhamma.gift/sn/sn12/index.html#sn12.15))

“Everything **exists**”: this, Kaccāna, is one extreme.

**2. *hoti* can also mean “exists”.**

‘**hoti** tathāgato paraṃ maraṇā’ ([MN63](https://s.dhamma.gift/mn/mn63/index.html))

“A Tathāgata **exists** after death.”

**3. *hoti* is not only for ideas and general statements.** It also describes what is present right now, as in *sati paccupaṭṭhitā hoti* above.

**4. In the first and second person √as is a simple “am” and “are”.** There is no sense of “there is” in *n’eso’ham’asmi*, “I am not this” (see [Irregular verb √as (be)](11_root_as.md)).

**5. *atthi* does not always follow the number of its subject.** “There are” should be *santi*, but *atthi* is often used with a plural subject, as in *puttā matthi* above.

**atthi**, bhikkhave, aññeva dhammā ([DN1](https://s.dhamma.gift/dn/dn1/index.html))

**There are**, monks, other things.

**6. English hides the difference.** Both verbs, and the sentences without a verb, all become “is” in a natural translation.

When reading, try “there is” for *atthi* and “is (something)” or “becomes” for *hoti* first. Then let the context decide.
