# Verbal Roots (dhātu)

Almost all verbs are formed from a root.

We are using the square root symbol “ √ ” to indicate root.

Some common roots are:

√kar (do)

√bhū (be)

√car (walk, move)

√car (act, behave)

√har (carry, take)

√jīv (live)

**If you understand the roots**, and how verbs, participles and nouns get formed from [prefixes] + roots (bases) + suffixes, you will understand more than half of the Pāḷi language.

## How do verbs get formed?

**Root + Conjugational Sign \> Base**

The root together with the conjugational sign is called the base/stem

root + conjugational sign = **base/stem**

√car + a = **cara**

**[Prefix(es)] + Base + Verbal ending \> Verb**

cara + ti \> carati (he walks)

cara + si \> carasi (you walk)

pari + cara + āmi \> paricarāmi (I walk around)

## Root Groups & Conjugational Signs

There are 7 or **8** groups of roots in Pāḷi (depending on the grammar tradition you are following).

These roots are grouped together because the verbs derived from them have similar formations.

| **\#** | **conjugational sign** | **example** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | a | √bandh (bind) + a \> bandha / bandha + ti \> bandhati (he ties) |
| 2 | ṃa | √rudh (obstruct) + ṃa \> ru**ṃ**dha \> ru**n**dha / rundha + ti \> rundhati (he obstructs) |
| 3 | ya | √div (shine) + ya \> di**bb**a / dibba + ti \> dibbati (he shines) |
| 4 | ṇā (ṇo[^8]) (after vowel) / uṇā (after consonant) | √su (hear) + ṇā \> suṇā / suṇā + ti \> suṇāti (he hears) / √ap (reach) + uṇā \> apuṇā / pa + apuṇā + ti \> pāpuṇāti (he reaches) |
| 5 | nā | √ji (conquer) + nā \> jinā / jinā + ti \> jināti (he conquers) |
| 6 | ṇhā | √gah (grasp) + ṇhā \> gaṇhā / gaṇhā + ti \> gaṇhāti (he grabs) |
| 7 | o | √kar (do) + o \> karo / karo + ti \> karoti (he does, makes) |
| 8 | \*e, \*aya | √cur (steal) + \*e \> c**o**re / core + ti \> coreti (he steals) / √cur (steal) + \*aya \> c**o**raya / coraya + ti \> corayati (he steals) |

[^8]: less common
